## What the evidence says

Teaching at the Right Level was developed by the Indian nonprofit Pratham and has been tested in randomized evaluations since 2001. J-PAL counts six randomized evaluations in seven Indian states and says the approach has produced some of the largest effects rigorously measured in education. In Uttar Pradesh, short learning camps doubled the number of children who could read a paragraph or story.

The evaluations also show where it fails. In Bihar and Uttarakhand, government teachers did not adopt the method, even after well-received training. It worked when it had protected time and support. In Haryana, teachers gave it a dedicated hour a day with mentoring from trained government staff, and language scores rose by 0.15 standard deviations. In Uttar Pradesh, intensive 40-day camps run by Pratham volunteers during school hours, plus summer camps, raised language scores by 0.70 standard deviations. J-PAL notes that training alone or materials alone did not improve learning.

At scale in Zambia the gains are smaller. A randomized trial of the government-run version, Catch Up, in 273 schools found gains of 0.10 standard deviations in literacy and 0.15 in numeracy after two years. Only about a third of students attended a remedial class on a given day, largely because many were absent, had dropped out or had moved schools. Students fully exposed to the program later scored 0.14 standard deviations higher in language and 0.11 in math on grade 7 exams.

In 2023 the Global Education Evidence Advisory Panel, convened by the World Bank and partners, listed teaching by learning level among its “Great Buys,” its top tier for cost and impact. The grade is strong because many rigorous trials agree that the approach raises basic skills when it is well implemented. How large the effect is depends heavily on delivery.

## Where it sits on the depth scale

Charitology places it at **prevention** (depth 2). It gives children who have fallen behind dedicated time at their own level, so that they don’t leave primary school without basic reading and math. When a ministry runs it through its own teachers, as Zambia does, it moves toward structure. Even then it works alongside the grade-based syllabus rather than replacing it.
