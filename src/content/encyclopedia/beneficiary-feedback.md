A business that ignores its customers soon finds out: sales fall. A charity that ignores the people it serves may never find out, because those people are not the ones paying. **Beneficiary feedback** is the practice of asking them anyway, in a systematic way, and changing the work in response.

## Why the signal is missing

In a 2013 article, Fay Twersky, Phil Buchanan and Valerie Threlfall argued that funders and nonprofits consult experts and crowds but rarely ask the people they serve. They pointed to the reasons: the people served often have few alternatives, funders rather than beneficiaries pay the provider, and incentives favor the funder’s view. So a flawed program can continue for years. This is the [Missing Feedback Loop](/diagnosis/missing-feedback-loop/) seen from the ground.

## What listening can show

The authors drew on fields that already listen. In health care, they cited research in which patients’ perceptions of their care, especially nursing care, predicted clinical quality and lower inpatient death rates for heart attack patients. In education, the YouthTruth initiative had gathered feedback from about 142,000 students, and 98 percent of participating school leaders said they used or planned to use the results. Their broader point was that the views of the people served can be a leading indicator, showing a problem before it shows up in outcomes.

They were also clear about the limits. Feedback is expensive to collect. People with low incomes or low literacy are hard to survey well, and people may fear that criticism will cost them help. Feedback works best alongside other evidence, not instead of it.

## Who is building it

Feedback Labs supports funders and nonprofits to listen to people and act on what they hear. Fund for Shared Insight, a national funder collaborative that ran for 12 years before concluding in 2026, launched Listen4Good, a program offering nonprofits tools and coaching to collect and use feedback. The Trust-Based Philanthropy Project counts “solicit and act on feedback” among its six practices, applied to the relationship between funders and grantees.

## The open question

Feedback that is collected but never moves money only half closes the loop. What would let the people served send a signal that changes where money goes, the way prices do in a market? That is [Open Problem 5, Closing the Feedback Loop](/open-problems/closing-the-feedback-loop/). [Participatory grantmaking](/record/encyclopedia/participatory-grantmaking/) and [cash transfers](/record/interventions/cash-transfers/), where recipients choose what to buy, are two partial answers.
