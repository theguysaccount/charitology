## What it looks like

A funding process can set priorities before the people affected have a say. Listening and participatory grantmaking initiatives document attempts to change that pattern. The sources here do not measure how often it occurs across charity.

## Why it happens

When funding authority sits with donors or grant committees, the people served may lack formal decision rights. This is the [Missing Feedback Loop](/diagnosis/missing-feedback-loop/) seen from the other side: even when the people served know what works, their knowledge may have no reliable route into funding decisions. Humanity’s Checklist calls this a gatekeeper problem. The hypothesis is that concentrated decision rights can exclude useful ideas.

A growing movement is working on the fix. Feedback Labs supports listening, and Fund for Shared Insight worked on that goal until it concluded in 2026. Its website is now an archive. Participatory grantmaking also gives community members a role in decisions about grants.

## No one is to blame

The issue is how decision rights are shared and whose knowledge reaches a decision. Motives cannot be inferred from a funding structure.

## What we don’t know yet

Do community-led programs outperform top-down ones on the same outcome, and by how much? That is [Open Problem 14, the Agency Premium](/open-problems/the-agency-premium/).
