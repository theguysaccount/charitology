## What it looks like

Decisions about what a community needs are usually made far from that community: in foundation boardrooms, donor meetings and grant committees. The people who will live with the results are surveyed, sometimes, after the plan is set.

## Why it happens

The money and the decisions sit together, and the people served have neither. This is the [Missing Feedback Loop](/diagnosis/missing-feedback-loop/) seen from the other side: even when the people served know what works, there is no channel that turns their knowledge into funding decisions. Humanity’s Checklist calls this a gatekeeper problem. A few hands decide what gets built, so most good ideas never ship.

A growing movement is working on the fix. Feedback Labs and Fund for Shared Insight push organizations to listen to the people they serve, and participatory grantmaking puts community members on the panels that decide where grants go.

## No one is to blame

Funders want to be careful with money, and careful usually means keeping decisions close. The system just doesn’t treat the people served as experts.

## What we don’t know yet

Do community-led programs outperform top-down ones on the same outcome, and by how much? That is [Open Problem 14, the Agency Premium](/open-problems/the-agency-premium/).
