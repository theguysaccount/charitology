## What it looks like

A food bank can show you the meals it served tonight. An organization changing the policies that cause hunger can show you a draft bill, a coalition meeting and a decade of work ahead. The first is easy to fund. The second often isn’t.

## Why it happens

Relief is visible, immediate and easy to credit to a single gift. Work on sources is slow, shared and hard to measure. Most of the other conditions push the same way: [stories](/diagnosis/story-bias/) favor relief, [restricted grants](/diagnosis/restriction-reflex/) favor projects with countable outputs, and [short funding cycles](/diagnosis/short-horizon/) favor quick results.

Humanity’s Checklist makes the larger argument plainly. Most of the world’s good work fights problems one at a time, while the problems grow from a few shared sources: the story we tell, how we coordinate, what we can picture, what our feeds reward, what we admire and who gets to build. Treating symptoms alone is “putting Band-Aids on an infection.”

## This is a hypothesis

Charitology’s founding hypothesis is that the charity system as a whole is weighted toward symptoms. **Nobody has measured this.** No public dataset classifies the world’s giving by [source depth](/method/#depth): relief, repair, prevention, structure, source. Measuring it is [Open Problem 1, the Depth Census](/open-problems/the-depth-census/). If the census finds the mix is already balanced, this condition comes off the list. That is what makes it science.

## No one is to blame

Relief is not a lesser good. People need to eat tonight, and relief is the only thing that helps them tonight. The question is the mix. A system that funds only relief will be funding the same relief forever.
