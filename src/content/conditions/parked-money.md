## What it looks like

Donor-advised funds held about **$326 billion** in 2024, according to the Annual DAF Report 2025. A donor who puts money into one gets the tax deduction right away, even though the money may reach a working charity years later. Private foundations, for their part, generally must pay out at least 5 percent of their assets each year.

## Why it’s debated

Both sides have numbers. Defenders point out that donor-advised funds granted $64.9 billion to charities in 2024, a payout rate of 25.3 percent, far above the foundation minimum. Critics point to the size of the pool and to accounts that rarely grant at all. Foundations argue that holding assets lets them give forever. Critics argue that today’s problems compound while endowments wait, and that the 5 percent floor often works as a ceiling.

## No one is to blame

Saving to give later can be wise, especially if better opportunities are coming. The trouble is that the system has no view on when money should move, so the default is whatever is easiest.

## What we don’t know yet

What is the best rate to spend philanthropic money now versus later? That is [Open Problem 12, the Payout Question](/open-problems/the-payout-question/).
