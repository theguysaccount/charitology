## What it looks like

People give where they live, worship and went to school. That giving builds real communities. It also means a dollar often goes where it is most familiar rather than where it does the most. GiveWell’s models of its top charities have typically estimated an average cost of $3,000 to $5,500 to save a life, mostly through health programs in low-income countries.

## Why it’s debated

This is a moral question as much as an empirical one, and serious people disagree. Peter Singer argued in 1972 that distance makes no moral difference: if you could save a drowning child in front of you at small cost, you should save a child far away at the same cost. Care ethicists and many religious traditions answer that we have special duties to the people close to us, and that local giving builds the trust and community a good society needs.

Charitology doesn’t settle this for anyone. It treats the *information gap* as the condition: most givers have never seen an honest comparison between giving near and giving far, so they can’t choose with their eyes open.

## No one is to blame

Caring for your own community is a virtue. The condition is only that the system rarely shows people the alternatives.

## What we don’t know yet
