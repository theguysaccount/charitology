## What it looks like

Rocket science has a discipline. So does brain surgery, and so does mixing a cocktail. Philanthropy has scholars too: Indiana University’s Lilly Family School of Philanthropy, established in 2012, describes itself as the first school dedicated to the study and teaching of philanthropy. There are evaluators, consultants and advisors.

What doesn’t exist yet is a shared, practical craft of deciding where generosity does the most good, across every cause and every method, with a name, a method, training and standards that practitioners hold each other to. In October 2026 even the obvious name for it, *charitology*, was available to register for about ten dollars.

## Why it happens

Doing good has always been treated as a matter of the heart rather than a craft. Each cause grew its own experts, and nobody’s job was to study the system that connects them. The [other fourteen conditions](/diagnosis/) are what a system looks like when no one is trained to steer it.

## This is a hypothesis

Charitology’s founding claim is that a profession would change where money goes, the way medicine changed how illness is treated. That hasn’t been tested. Testing it is [Open Problem 17, the Profession Question](/open-problems/the-profession-question/).

## No one is to blame

You can’t practice a craft nobody has named. That is the point of naming it.
