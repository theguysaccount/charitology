## What it is

The Cause Census maps every cause area Charitology tracks, from global health to arts and culture. For each cause it aims to answer the three questions that 80,000 Hours sets out in its framework for comparing global problems: how big is the problem, how neglected is it and how much would more effort solve? The census adds a fourth question of its own: where on the [depth scale](/method/#depth) does the money already going to this cause act?

## How it works

The census starts from the [Cause Atlas](/record/cause-atlas/), which places 17 cause areas on five dimensions, such as whom a cause protects and how certain its results are. Those positions are editorial judgments, and the data file says so. The census would add sourced estimates on top: how many people or beings a problem affects, how much money already goes to it and what the evidence says about progress per dollar.

Every estimate would carry its assumptions, and scale needs the most care. A problem’s size depends on what you count: lives, rights, species, future people or the health of a community. So the census would report scale through more than one [moral framework](/method/#frameworks) and show where the ranking changes.

## Why it matters for the system

The [Story Bias](/diagnosis/story-bias/) sends money where attention goes, and attention follows faces and headlines more than size. A public census would put the size of each problem next to the attention it gets, so a giver can see the gap for themselves.

The depth question treats the [Symptom Trap](/diagnosis/symptom-trap/). Within each cause, the census would ask how much money goes to relief and how much to work on sources. That is the [Depth Census](/open-problems/the-depth-census/) carried out one cause at a time. Nobody has measured it yet, which is exactly why it is worth measuring.

## Risks and limits

Numbers can hide value judgments. Neglectedness can only be measured for resources someone tracks, such as grants, so informal giving and volunteer time are easy to miss. The census would label each number with its source, its year and the judgment behind it.

## What success looks like

Within five years, every cause area has sourced estimates with stated assumptions and the depth mix is estimated for at least five causes. Funders cite the census when they explain where their money goes.
