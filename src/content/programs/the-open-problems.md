## What it is

In 1900 the mathematician David Hilbert published 23 unsolved problems, and they shaped a century of mathematics. Charitology’s [Open Problems](/open-problems/) do the same for doing good. Each one turns a condition from the [Diagnosis](/diagnosis/) into a question precise enough that someone could answer it, with a stated test for what would count as progress.

## How it works

Every problem is a public page and a file in the repository. Anyone can work on one. Approaches, datasets and partial results go into GitHub issues tagged `open-problem`, where they are discussed in public. When a result holds up under review, the problem’s status moves from `open` to `partial` or `solved`, and the [Progress Report](/record/progress-report/) records it.

The list itself is open to revision. A new problem gets in when it names a condition it would treat and states what would count as progress.

## Why it matters for the system

A field is defined as much by its questions as by its answers. Today the hardest questions in giving belong to nobody: they fall between disciplines, causes and funders. Naming them gives researchers a target, funders a portfolio and newcomers a place to start, which is the first step toward [the missing profession](/diagnosis/missing-profession/).

## What success looks like

Within five years, at least five problems have published partial results that change how a meaningful amount of money is given, and the list has been revised at least once because the field learned something.
