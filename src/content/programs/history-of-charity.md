## What it is

Giving has a long record, and much of it has never been read as evidence. The History of Charity program studies that record: which efforts solved a problem for good, which ones failed and why, and what each one teaches today. Each study becomes an entry in the [Historical Case Studies](/record/case-studies/).

## How it works

Every case follows one format: what happened, where and when, where it acted on the [depth scale](/method/#depth), and the lessons that carry over. Cases are labeled as successes, failures or mixed results, and every claim is sourced. The first case is the eradication of smallpox, which the World Health Organization judged complete in 1980.

History also holds earlier attempts at a science of giving, and Charitology studies them as closely as any other case. Starting with the London Charity Organization Society, founded in 1869, reformers tried to put charity on a scientific footing. They investigated and registered applicants, kept central records and brought local charities together to coordinate their work. Their methods later fed into the casework of modern social work. They also screened applicants by asking whether each one was worthy or unworthy. Both halves of that legacy are lessons. The first shows that careful method can grow into a profession. The second is a warning Charitology takes seriously: study the system, and never grade the people in it.

## Why it matters for the system

History is the only place where the long run has already happened. The [Short Horizon](/diagnosis/short-horizon/) means most giving is judged within a year or two. A case study can show what a twenty-year effort achieved, or what a quick success cost later.

History also works against the [Silence About Failure](/diagnosis/silence-about-failure/). Failures that were never published at the time can still be studied from archives, so their lessons are learned once instead of again and again.

## Risks and limits

The surviving records may come mostly from funders and institutions rather than the people they served, and famous successes may be better documented than quiet failures. The program seeks sources from the communities involved, and each case should say how complete its record is.

## What success looks like

Within five years, the case studies cover successes, failures and mixed results from many regions and centuries, and Charitology’s methods cite the historical evidence behind them.
