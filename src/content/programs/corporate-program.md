## What it is

Companies shape giving in two ways: through their own grants and through programs that help employees give, such as payroll giving and gift matching. Corporations accounted for about 7 percent of the estimated $617.2 billion given to U.S. nonprofits in 2025, according to Giving USA. The Corporate Program would redesign corporate and workplace giving around what gifts achieve.

## How it works

The program would start from a short set of draft principles:

- **Match without strings.** Match gifts for general support at least as readily as gifts tied to projects, and offer multi-year matches.
- **Report results, not totals.** Show employees what their pooled giving achieved, not only how much was given.
- **Show the lenses.** Offer the [Values Compass](/tools/values-compass/) and depth information at the moment people give, so they can see their choices across frameworks.
- **Celebrate the best-aimed gift.** Recognize gifts for what they did, not for how big or visible they were.
- **Count time well.** Use [Time Charitology](/programs/time-charitology/) to design volunteer programs around what organizations actually need.

A pilot company would apply the principles for a year and publish what changed, including what didn’t work.

## Why it matters for the system

A company’s grant terms and matching rules apply to every gift that passes through them. If those rules favor project grants over general support, they build the [Restriction Reflex](/diagnosis/restriction-reflex/) into all of those gifts at once. Rewritten, the same rules can make flexible funding the default instead.

Companies also decide what to celebrate. Recognizing gifts for what they achieved, rather than how large or public they were, points the [Prestige Economy](/diagnosis/prestige-economy/) at results.

## Risks and limits

Workplace giving belongs to employees, and a redesign shouldn’t push anyone toward particular causes. The principles widen what employees see. They don’t narrow what they may choose. Business goals such as marketing can also pull a program toward visible gifts, so pilots would publish their results either way.

## What success looks like

Within five years, at least ten companies have adopted the principles and published their results, and the share of matched gifts that go to general support has grown at those companies.
