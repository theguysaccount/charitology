## The debate

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy maps the main positions. For Kant, only rational persons have intrinsic moral worth, and we owe animals duties only indirectly, because cruelty harms our humanity. Peter Singer, following Bentham, holds that the capacity to suffer is what counts and that equal interests deserve equal weight regardless of species, though he weighs interests case by case. Tom Regan argues that animals are subjects of a life with rights that can’t be overridden simply because humans benefit. Relational and care-based views hold that moral status depends on our relationships with animals rather than on a property they have.

Science has changed the question of which animals can suffer. The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness, released in April 2024 and showing more than 600 signatures, says there is strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds, and at least a realistic possibility in all vertebrates and many invertebrates, including cephalopod mollusks, decapod crustaceans and insects. It calls it irresponsible to ignore that possibility in decisions affecting an animal. The United Kingdom’s Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 counts cephalopod mollusks and decapod crustaceans as animals, alongside vertebrates.

Comparing species is the hardest part. Rethink Priorities’ 2023 welfare range estimates put a chicken’s median capacity for welfare at about a third of a human’s and a shrimp’s at about 3 percent. Their 90 percent intervals run from near zero to roughly the human level or above, and the authors call the figures placeholders that rest on contested philosophical assumptions.

## Where Charitology stands

How much animals count is a value question, and the frameworks Charitology uses answer it differently. Charitology doesn’t settle it. The evidence question is narrower, and it is moving: scientific support for sentience now reaches well beyond mammals, while estimates of how to compare one species with another remain very uncertain. A giver who uses one framework without noticing may never see this choice at all, which is [the Single Lens](/diagnosis/single-lens/).
