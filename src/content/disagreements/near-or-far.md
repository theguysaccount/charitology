## The debate

In 1972 Peter Singer argued that if you could save a child drowning in front of you at the cost of ruining your clothes, you should, and that a child dying far away has the same claim on you. Many people find the argument hard to refute and hard to live by.

The other side is just as serious. Some care-based and religious accounts hold that the people closest to us have special claims: we owe our children more than strangers, and communities are built by people who show up for each other.

## Where Charitology stands

Charitology doesn’t settle this for anyone. It treats the information gap as the problem it can fix. Comparisons should make their evidence, uncertainty and assumptions about special obligations clear. The sources here do not establish how many donors lack such information.
