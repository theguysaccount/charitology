# Charity networks and leadership outreach plan

Status: proposed discovery process, October 28, 2026 UTC. No message, invitation, membership application or pilot enrollment has been sent or created. This is an AI-assisted preparation document. Its timing, batch size and interview format are editorial proposals, not tested outreach findings.

## Purpose and first batch

Learn how charity teams coordinate work and how networks already help them. Offer an inspectable public resource, then ask whether a short conversation or a voluntary, bounded software trial would be useful. The aim is to discover a problem worth solving, including learning that the current tools already do the job.

Propose a first batch of **four network organizers and four charity leaders**. This is a manageable learning batch, not a representative sample or a conversion target. Seek different organization stages, missions, geographies and available staff time. Do not infer an organization’s willingness, software needs or resources from its inclusion in a directory.

For charity teams, request the executive director, chief executive or a delegated operations/program lead. For networks, request the person responsible for member support, learning or community programs. Use the organization’s official general or partnership route and ask for appropriate routing. A current title or a public inbox does not establish personal consent or a promise of response. Do not infer email patterns, use beneficiary support channels, submit a pitch through a funding application or seek a member list.

A private, source-linked shortlist and not-contacted tracking sheet are prepared separately from the public directory. Private replies, names, meeting notes and contact preferences do not belong in this repository or a public issue. Each proposed target still needs a current route and fit check before any message is sent. Exclude routes that expressly reject unsolicited technology/product inquiries; Water For People’s [official contact page](https://www.waterforpeople.org/contact-us/) states this restriction and is excluded from the initial software outreach list.

## What to offer

1. The public [fundraising hub](https://charitology.org/for-charities/fundraising/): ten method guides, a page-only cost/follow-up planner and reviewed Markdown/calendar downloads.
2. The [networks directory](https://charitology.org/for-charities/networks/): selected annotated guides with official sources, access conditions and correction routes.
3. A short fictional demonstration of [Charity OS](https://os.charitology.org), if requested. State its current scope and limits. An account, data migration or real donor/beneficiary record is not needed for a first conversation.

Lead with the system and the work, not a judgment of the charity. Explain that the public source entries are editorial drafts, the research and OS are still developing, and no fundraising improvement, efficiency saving, partnership or independent review is established.

Subject: Could we learn from one difficult handover at [organization]?

I would like to understand what already works and where information or responsibility gets lost, rather than assume you need another platform.

This [relevant public guide] shows the kind of resource we are making. It is an editorial draft, not a claim that we have proved better results. Would a twenty-minute conversation be useful? There is no need to share private records, create an account or commit to a trial. If another person or route is more appropriate, a pointer would help.

Thank you,
[sender name]
Charitology · https://charitology.org
hello@charitology.org

Before sending, replace every placeholder with inspected facts and the owner’s chosen wording. Do not imply a prior relationship or fabricate praise.

Subject: A practical resource for [network’s stated audience] — feedback welcome

Hello [network] team,

I’m [sender name], working on Charitology, an open project studying the systems around charitable work. We have prepared [specific guide] for [audience supported by the official source], including source links and limits.

Your published work on [specific relevant activity] seems connected to a question we are exploring: [one bounded question]. Would the person responsible for member support or learning be willing to tell us whether this is useful, missing something or duplicating a resource you already recommend?

We would welcome a short conversation if it fits your work. We are not asking for a membership list or assuming permission to promote anything to your community. You can also suggest a correction to your directory entry without taking part in an interview or trial.

Thank you,
[sender name]
Charitology · https://charitology.org

## Conversation guide and optional trial

- Walk through one recurring job: what starts it, who acts, what is handed over and how completion is checked.
- Ask what their current tools do well and what cannot be disrupted.
- Ask where staff time is spent, how follow-up is assigned and what remains unknown. Avoid requesting personal records.
- Ask how a peer network or shared guide currently helps, and who is missing from that feedback.
- If useful, agree one fictional software example, one responsible contact, a time limit and a clear stop decision. Check export, access and recovery before considering any later real data.
- Agree separately what notes may be retained and whether any quote, organization name or case study may be published. No recording by default.

The proposed trial is voluntary discovery. Define success with the participant around a specific task; a completed task or favorable comment does not establish causal benefit or mission impact. Do not replace their accounting, safeguarding or specialist case system.

## Contact and follow-up workflow

1. Check the current official page, relevant role, allowed contact purpose and any no-solicitation rule. Record the source and actual check date.
2. Prepare one personalized draft per organization, with one source-grounded question and a relevant public artifact. Check facts, placeholders and claims.
3. Present the exact recipient/route, sender and message for the owner’s explicit authorization before sending. This request authorized planning and publication, not outbound messages.
4. After an authorized send, record its actual provider/reference and status privately. A draft is not sent; sent is not delivered; delivered is not read or interested. Keep unknowns unknown.
5. Propose at most one short follow-up after seven to ten business days, if appropriate for the route. Confirm actual contact history and preferences first; stop on a decline or request not to contact. This is a planning suggestion, not evidence of optimal timing.
6. Send no automated sequence, newsletter subscription, calendar invitation or community post as a side effect. Obtain the relevant organizer’s permission before any community promotion.

## Learning and decisions

Use separate counts for drafts reviewed, authorized attempts, confirmed deliveries, responses, conversations and explicitly agreed trials. Retain each denominator and observation window. Four conversations are qualitative feedback, not a survey of the sector. Record contradictory feedback and useful reasons not to proceed.

After the first batch, review which problems recur, which groups were absent, whether a public resource should change and whether any trial has a clear owner and scope. Publish only permissioned, aggregate learning. A useful outcome may be a better directory entry or a referral to an existing tool rather than adoption of Charity OS.

The shared OS can hold manually entered organization-level Resources and Projects/tasks after sign-in and organization choice. This plan adds no automatic campaign, contact import, donor email delivery, provider access or OS record.
