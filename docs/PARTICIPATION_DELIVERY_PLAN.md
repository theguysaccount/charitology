# Participation delivery plan

Frozen pre-implementation contract · October 26, 2026 · AI-assisted planning

This is the next bounded slice after [participation bookings](PARTICIPATION_BOOKINGS_PLAN.md). It proposes real operational delivery through the existing Charity OS mail relay and authorized Resend account. It does not establish that delivery code, enabled configuration, participant use or inbox receipt exists. This planning pass changes no runtime, database, provider, secret, account, DNS or production preference. No new paid service is proposed.

## Outcome and boundary

A verified participant may explicitly enable operational email updates for one of their own linked participations. An authorized organization administrator can preview and deliberately activate a bounded set of current, eligible confirmations. Subsequent relevant waitlist, confirmation and cancellation changes can create delivery intents under that organization policy. A fixed, minimal message directs its single recipient to the clean signed-in participant portal. The portal remains the source of current state.

This slice delivers updates. It does not add newsletter enrollment, marketing, arbitrary staff mail, automatic selection, reminders before an occurrence, bookings through email, sensitive casework, attachments, calendar invitations, payments or a new public read/unsubscribe bearer. Legacy and recovered occupancy never acquire an address, access grant or email consent from their status. No actual person, human review or attendance is inferred from tests.

The frozen first template has a fixed subject, **Your Charity OS participation update**, and fixed HTML/plain-text text: **There is an update in your Charity OS participant portal. Sign in to review the current details.** Its only action is the exact configured origin plus `/participant`, with no query or fragment. Include the established sender/reply address and postal footer. Omit occurrence title, organization name, schedule, answers, contact IDs, request IDs, booking IDs, staff reasons and private receipts. A later stale message therefore directs the person to current state without asserting an obsolete confirmation. This is an agreed design, not an implemented template.

## Access and fresh delivery consent

Delivery requires all of these facts at activation and again immediately before the provider attempt:

1. The participation belongs to the current organization and has the exact causal state/revision represented by the notification. It is managed, not recovered, and its source has not been replaced by an untrusted restore.
2. There is exactly one active own-request link for the participation's associated intake. The server resolves its user; staff cannot select an arbitrary address or match a contact by email. A public receipt alone has no delivery authority.
3. Better Auth still records that account's email as verified. Normalize it with the existing participant email rule and compare it with the immutable verified recipient binding of the delivery consent. A changed email stops delivery rather than redirecting it. Contact-record email and intake answers are not recipient sources.
4. A current, explicit per-participation operational-email consent is enabled and matches its version, account/link and recipient hash. Collection, claim, attendance and waitlist consent are separate facts. An offline assertion cannot enable this preference.
5. The organization policy and both service-level participation delivery gates are enabled. The event is not stale, superseded, cancelled, expired, already accepted, blocked or unknown.
6. Applicable provider suppression is absent, sender/tracking readiness passes and the bounded existing relay rates allow the operation. Failure to inspect required readiness fails closed.

**Frozen consent rule:** the base release's checkbox says Preference only — no messages sent in this release. Do not silently upgrade that historical choice into live delivery permission. Add a new immutable delivery-consent copy version, server timestamp and verified recipient binding. The participant explicitly chooses **Email me operational updates about this participation. I can turn them off here. This does not subscribe me to The Charitology Letter.** Start unchecked when no current delivery consent exists. Enabling the feature or an administrator selecting a preview cannot make that choice for them.

Keep delivery preference consent independent from attendance intent. A confirmed participation retains its frozen booking terms; the existing no-rescheduling rule does not permit a new attendance-consent form on a confirmed entry. A separate own-link preference command can deliberately enable email for that unchanged confirmed participation after showing its frozen public details and the new email copy. It cannot amend the booking, transfer the recipient or invent a new approval. For requested/waitlisted entries, new allocation still requires the booking contract's exact source-revision and terms checks. Disabling email requires only current own-link authority and expected revision; it never requires a future/open occurrence, fresh staff approval or an available ordinary quota.

Do not claim that consent withdrawal can recall an email already handed to a provider. If withdrawal/unlink commits before the final eligibility check, do not start the attempt. A change after that check can race with external transport. Keep the interval bounded, cancel unsent work and explain that an already attempted message may still arrive. Do not hold the tenant allocation lock across a slow provider call and thereby block safe cancellation or opt-out.

## Deliberate activation without backlog delivery

Use a global `OS_PARTICIPATION_DELIVERY_ENABLED=false` gate, a separate relay gate and a revisioned, strict-tenant organization delivery policy. The gate names are proposals. Add a separate controlled-recipient mode, defaulting on until the owned-inbox exercise passes. Store the actual controlled recipient privately and do not return it through health, preview or status responses. Production credentials remain server-only and absent from test/fork environments.

An administrator with current organization authority prepares a preview of at most **20 selected current confirmed participations**. Each row must have fresh delivery consent, an active verified own-link recipient, current managed authority and an unchanged frozen confirmation. The preview reports eligible/ineligible counts and safe reasons. It exposes no raw addresses or private answers. A private operator may inspect their own identity through established account controls; a preview is not a recipient export.

The preview binds organization-policy revision, participation revision, own-link identity/revocation version, delivery-consent version/time/hash, current recipient hash, template version and safe payload hash. Use an existing bounded private preview with a ten-minute expiry and a canonical review hash, or an equivalent explicitly validated preview mechanism. A separate CSRF-protected commit with expected versions, the exact review hash and a random command key rechecks every selected row under the organization lock. A stale or invalid selection fails as a whole; no partial activation is hidden.

Commit creates **new current-state snapshot intents** for the selected confirmations. It never changes an old paused event to pending, sends a history of earlier changes or resets a previous accepted/unknown intent. Existing paused base rows remain non-sendable/cancelled provenance. The policy records an activation boundary. Only later causal changes admitted under active policy and current delivery consent may queue future notification events. Requested state alone does not need a message; waitlist, confirmation and cancellation are proposed supported transitions. An administrator may disable the policy at any time without cancelling places.

An opted-in participant enabling email for an already confirmed place does not itself send a message. They can see the saved preference immediately; an authorized current-state preview or later relevant change supplies a separate delivery intent. There is no hidden welcome/bulk campaign. This avoids treating preference writes as arbitrary send commands.

## Causal outbox and dispatch contract

Extend the existing participation outbox deliberately, retaining its strict tenant FORCE RLS and composite references. Do not copy events into `os_jobs` and maintain competing delivery states. Keep the unique organization/participation/event revision/purpose/channel identity and add an explicit snapshot/transition reason where a preview creates a new current-state intent. Prevent repeated activation from generating repeated notices for the same participation revision, delivery-consent version and template.

| Retained field group | Planned contents |
| --- | --- |
| Causal identity | Existing row/organization/participation IDs, event revision, purpose, channel, template/version and activation or transition identity. |
| Permission binding | Opaque own-link/user reference, consent version/time and recipient hash. Resolve the address in memory immediately before transport; do not persist it in an outbox payload. Hashes and opaque references remain private personal-data references. |
| Immutable intent | Stable relay key identity, canonical payload hash, first-attempt time and attempted template/origin version. A mutable organization title must not change an old keyed payload. The proposed fixed template makes this smaller. |
| Work state | `paused`, `pending`, `leased`, `accepted`, `delivered`, `suppressed`, `failed`, `unknown`, `cancelled`; attempts, lease owner/expiry, next attempt time and bounded safe outcome code. A transient defer before any provider attempt is a pending scheduling fact, not provider acceptance. |
| Private feedback | Safe outcome code and provider event time in the OS. Provider IDs and detailed bound feedback/dedupe references stay in the existing private mail-service intent; no raw webhook body, address, private subject or provider error string in OS browser output/logs. |

The dispatcher acquires the tenant lock to choose and lease bounded eligible work, supersedes stale unattempted rows, and commits that lease before external I/O. Use a lease-owner nonce and conditional finalization so an expired worker cannot overwrite a newer saved outcome. Recheck current recipient/consent/link/state after acquiring the lease, immediately before invoking the relay. No external call occurs inside the booking confirmation transaction. A mail failure never rolls back, duplicates or frees a place.

Follow the existing shared **45-second** dispatcher budget and bounded tenant visitation. The frozen limit is **20 work/status operations total per invocation**, including existing jobs, participation sends and participation reconciliation. Process existing jobs first, then visit participation tenants round-robin; stop before the remaining network budget is exhausted. New participation delivery is explicitly dispatched; the current generic job fallback cannot send an unrecognized payload. Dispatch schedule remains the existing daily maintenance cadence, with an optional deliberate bounded operator batch under the same limit. This is not an exact-time or emergency-notification guarantee.

If the policy is disabled, consent withdrawn, link revoked, recipient changed or a later relevant booking revision supersedes an unattempted intent, atomically mark it cancelled without transport. Do not resurrect it when preferences are re-enabled. A later deliberate state/consent snapshot needs its own causal identity.

**Terminal participation cancellation is distinct from a privacy action.** It may create at most one current terminal-state notice if the pre-existing explicit delivery opt-in remains enabled, the own link remains active and the verified recipient binding still matches. The cancellation command does not create or renew that opt-in. Pending work for earlier waitlist/confirmation states is superseded. At dispatch, a cancelled participation is eligible only for that matching current cancellation revision; it is never required to be open/approved again. A missing journal entry because the safe-cancellation reserve was exhausted is not recreated automatically. Already accepted earlier mail remains history and cannot be recalled. Cancellation always commits even when its notice cannot be queued or sent.

Request withdrawal, answer erasure, permanent unlink, email opt-out, account/privacy recovery and policy disable do **not** create a fresh notification. In particular, do not send an erasure/withdrawal/unlink confirmation email or a message containing that private fact. Unlink/opt-out removes unsent delivery authority. **Answer erasure disables delivery consent and cancels unsent work**, retaining only minimal private withdrawal provenance; it does not remove the independent attendance intent or booking. Show that boundary before the action. Privacy actions cannot trigger a hidden terminal notice by routing through the booking sender.

## Relay purpose, status and uncertain acceptance

Add **`participation`** explicitly to relay validation, database constraints, rendering and tests. Its URL is exactly the configured OS origin plus `/participant`, with no query, fragment, credentials, alternate path or redirects. For this purpose, reject caller-supplied arbitrary title/message fields rather than rendering staff-authored content. Preserve the existing independent server bearer, Origin-header refusal, single recipient and 16 KiB request bound. The new purpose must not widen login/invitation/reminder URLs or expose a browser-to-relay credential.

Add an authenticated server-only **`POST /api/system/os-mail/status`** accepting the existing bounded intent-key identity in JSON. Its response contains only **`state`, `safeCode` and `providerEventAt`**, with a small standard success envelope. Never return recipient, token, message/payload, provider ID or raw provider error. It accepts neither a public email search nor a provider URL and cannot look up another key through a caller-supplied provider ID. Status/reconciliation never sends. The path is a proposed implementation name; the frozen authority is the existing independent server bearer plus exact intent key. Apply a durable **60 reads/minute global** cap on this authenticated status path; it consumes no send quota and remains within the shared 20-operation dispatch budget.

The relay should return typed machine outcomes alongside safe public copy, and the OS client must validate that JSON rather than treating any success response as delivery. Preserve the durable reservation committed before provider I/O. Retain the same native provider key for the one immutable attempt. Resend documents a **24-hour** idempotency cache for send-email requests; application intent must remain authoritative beyond that window. Never generate a new key because a lease expired or an old request timed out. [Primary idempotency contract](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/idempotency-keys).

| Observed boundary | OS action |
| --- | --- |
| Local eligibility/quota/readiness fails before any attempt | Keep/cancel/defer the same eligible intent as appropriate; no claimed acceptance. A definitive terminal rejection is visible. Do not let an operator Retry silently reset a durable failure. |
| Relay says not attempted and safely deferred | Bounded retry is allowed only under an explicit relay contract guaranteeing no provider attempt; preserve causal/payload/key identity and recheck eligibility. Current `limited`/`failed` behavior is terminal unless deliberately extended and tested. |
| Relay returns initial or duplicate accepted | Store accepted and sanitized known outcome; its provider identity stays private to the relay. Never send that intent again. |
| Relay/browser network or process completion is ambiguous | Store unknown, or reconcile the same key before considering any further operation. A relay `creating`/`unknown` stop never becomes a new-key retry. A crashed lease after a possible attempt is unknown, not automatically pending. |
| Status proves accepted | Recover the existing accepted result without sending. |
| Status has no evidence or provider identity | Remain unknown and show operator recovery guidance. Absence of a callback or a search result is not proof that no email was sent. No automatic resend after the cache window. |
| Known provider status can be read | Use bounded retrieval only for the provider ID already bound to that relay intent; expose safe outcomes only. Provider retrieval is a read, not permission to resend. [Primary retrieval contract](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/emails/retrieve-email). |

The recommended first release is conservative: no automated provider retry after an ambiguous attempt, and no new-key replacement for unknown history. A future explicit retry of a proven non-attempt must be designed as such, not inferred from generic HTTP 503. Killing a worker, losing a response and exhausting a lease all need tests.

## Signed feedback and suppression

Implement OS delivery feedback in the existing mail service before showing a Delivered status. Retain the current **raw-body** signature verification, bounded body and durable event-ID deduplication before processing. Bind each event to an existing `participation` relay intent using its recorded provider ID. If the provider response was lost before its ID was saved, an opaque HMAC correlation tag may recover that mapping only after the actual send/tag/webhook wire contract is tested; it must reveal no organization, user, participation, email or receipt. An unknown or unmatched callback cannot create consent, a new OS row, a booking or a send. [Primary verification contract](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/verify-webhooks-requests).

Record verified `sent`, `delivered`, delayed, failed, bounced, complained and suppressed facts separately with provider time. Reduce them deterministically: an older sent event cannot overwrite a recorded delivery or suppression; a later complaint/bounce is retained even after delivery. Missing/invalid timestamps cannot manufacture chronology. Signed `email.delivered` means the receiving mail server accepted the message; it does not establish inbox placement or reading. The documented event payload includes a provider email ID and optional tags. [Primary delivery event](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/emails/delivered), [event semantics](https://resend.com/docs/webhooks/event-types).

Provider feedback should first update the mail service's private intent state. The OS retrieves sanitized outcomes by authenticated status reconciliation, avoiding a new public OS callback or shared-database privilege. The mail webhook handler must process newsletter and OS effects atomically with deduplication: a successfully deduped newsletter event cannot prevent a failed OS update from being retried. Keep bounded minimal feedback references; do not store raw shared-team callback payloads or unrelated recipient data.

Before a participation attempt, inspect the existing team's suppression for this exact recipient through the fixed documented suppression endpoint. A documented absence permits continuation; an unknown/error does not. If the current Connect permission does not permit that read, report that concrete configuration gate before enabling this feature. Never remove a suppression or create/update a Contact to work around it. Team suppressions apply to transactional mail as well as broadcasts. Store an appropriate private blocked outcome and cancel unsent operational intents when verified hard-bounce/complaint/suppression feedback matches an existing recipient binding. [Suppression behavior](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/emails/email-suppressions), [exact-recipient read](https://resend.com/docs/api-reference/suppressions/get-suppression).

Newsletter subscription consent, Topic membership, native unsubscribe and operational preference remain separate. This slice neither creates nor resets newsletter state, Contact global flags, Topic/Segment membership or Broadcasts. It does not use an opted-out newsletter list as an operational mailing list. Preserve provider-wide suppression regardless of purpose. A provider suppression removal alone must not reactivate old queued work or restore operational consent; a new current safe activation is deliberate.

## Portal and staff behavior

The participant's own card has one plainly described operational-email preference, expected saved revision, a separate save action and a clear off state. Confirmed details remain frozen. A preference write never changes attendance intent, waitlist choice or allocation. Disabled/unavailable configuration is visible; do not call a paused preference working notifications. On a failed write retain the unsaved choice, and on stale state re-read with an explicit notice. No persistent local copy of private choices or receipts is introduced.

Turning updates off cancels eligible unsent work and stays available at full ordinary budget. Unlink immediately removes the recipient authority and cancels unsent work; it does not cancel the place. Request answer erasure disables delivery consent and cancels unsent work while leaving attendance intent and allocation unchanged. Show those boundaries before erasure and offer the independent Disable email updates action; do not infer a new address from an erased request. A person can disable email without erasing answers or cancelling participation. Restored records have no active links or delivery consent.

Staff see bounded counts and safe per-intent state for authorized program-scoped participations, never raw addresses or another program's roster. Administrators alone activate/disable organization delivery policy. The current own-link participant alone enables their address-bound consent. Staff cannot check that box on their behalf. A notification failure prompts Open portal / contact the organizer through the approved route; it does not imply the booking failed.

| UI label | Evidence required |
| --- | --- |
| Updates off / unavailable | Saved preference false, service policy disabled or no valid recipient authority. |
| Ready for a future update | Current delivery consent enabled; no send is implied. |
| Queued | A current eligible pending intent exists, with bounded dispatch still to occur. |
| Acceptance unknown | A possible provider attempt has no confirmed result. No automatic resend is promised. |
| Accepted by email provider | Durable relay/provider acceptance, including an accepted duplicate recovery. |
| Delivered to receiving mail server | Verified bound delivery feedback or validated provider retrieval. Never label Read or Inbox. |
| Blocked / failed / cancelled | Safe recorded transport outcome; participation state remains separate. |

Mail uses only the clean portal URL. Sign-in retains the established scanner-safe confirmation flow and allowlisted participant destination. Do not add an unsubscribe GET, anonymous status link, email-address lookup, receipt in mail or automatic login capability. Provider open/click tracking stays disabled and unknown tracking configuration blocks delivery. No read receipts or analytics are added.

## Budgets, safe cancellation and recovery

Keep the base **8,000 retained outbox rows / 8 MiB** cap and **2 KiB safe payload** cap, counting all new status/lease/feedback metadata in actual PostgreSQL row bytes and the existing **60 MiB** logical workspace budget. Existing 500-target/2 MiB and 2,000-participation/8 MiB bounds remain. Settings/previews and any new OS feedback records also count; avoid an unbounded second event store. Reuse the existing emergency reserve for minimal cancellation, preference withdrawal, policy disable and unlink audit. A full ordinary/outbox budget must still let those actions succeed, cancel unsent jobs and return an explicit notice if no new notification could be queued.

Use the existing relay's 200/day global and 10/recipient/hour durable caps rather than silently increasing paid usage; preserve capacity for authentication and existing operational purposes. Each attempted intent consumes the defined rate once. Duplicate/status reads do not become new sends. Existing jobs run first, participation tenants rotate round-robin and authenticated status reads have the separate 60/minute global durable cap. Do not claim that provider plan allowance guarantees these application rates or delivery.

Retain minimal terminal keyed tombstones **indefinitely in this first slice**, without automatic deletion. Existing relay intents are not automatically deleted by current cleanup. Preserve keyed identity, payload/recipient binding and terminal state beyond the provider cache; never forget accepted/unknown history and thereby permit resurrection. Keep additional feedback bounded and avoid retaining raw callback contents. A later compaction/retention policy needs separate review and a named operator; it is not an excuse to reset an old send identity.

Export private delivery provenance and safe outcomes within validated backups. Restore all policies disabled, intents non-sendable and recipient/consent authority absent. Provider IDs and old event tags are historical data, never send credentials or trusted new recipient grants. A restored accepted or unknown event cannot be resent. Keep the source archive honest and do not imply that erasure changes earlier private downloaded backups or provider retention. A later administrator activation must require current verified links, fresh delivery consent and a new bounded preview rather than replay old source history.

## Frozen implementation contract

The coordinator has approved the clean fixed notice and explicit bounded opt-in activation before delivery code. The bounded contract is:

1. **Minimal message:** fixed generic update subject/body and only the clean portal URL, without occurrence/organization details. This keeps state and private context in the portal.
2. **Fresh consent:** a new versioned live-delivery opt-in, including a separate command for confirmed participations. Historical preference-only choices never become send authority automatically.
3. **Activation:** disabled-by-default OS and relay gates, controlled-recipient mode, per-organization administrator preview/commit of at most20 current confirmations, plus future waitlist/confirmation/cancellation changes. No welcome email, requested-state message or automatic backlog flush.
4. **Terminal and privacy rules:** one current participation-cancellation notice may use retained explicit opt-in and an active verified link. Request withdrawal, erasure, unlink and preference/privacy changes never enqueue fresh mail; safe cancellation and opt-out cannot depend on transport or ordinary quota.
5. **Outcome contract:** extend the existing relay with bearer-and-existing-intent-key status returning state/safeCode/providerEventAt only, conservative unknown-stop semantics and signed delivery feedback. No raw recipient, token, payload or provider ID is returned. Any opaque callback correlation requires its actual wire-contract test. Proven pre-attempt defer semantics must be explicit; unknown is never an automatic resend.
6. **Privacy and operation limits:** answer erasure disables delivery consent and cancels unsent work without touching attendance. Retain row/byte and send caps, at most20 operations total per invocation with existing jobs first and participation tenants round-robin, separate durable60/minute global status cap, and indefinite minimal terminal keyed tombstones with no automatic deletion. Existing daily cadence is the initial schedule, with optional deliberate bounded operator dispatch.

This document is the frozen design, not implementation or delivery evidence. Existing user authorization covers work on the selected account/service, controlled owned-inbox testing and production release; it does not require a new approval question for reversible configuration within that scope. Actual provider ability, webhook subscriptions, configuration, tests and controlled inbox receipt still need to be verified and recorded as concrete acceptance gates. This planning task performs none of those changes. No new account or purchase is required.

## Frozen implementation interfaces

Recorded before delivery implementation on branch `participation-delivery`, based on booking merge `2fbfc75`. Existing own-user link RLS stays unchanged. Server-created live consent captures the authenticated user/link/version/recipient hash; recipient resolution temporarily applies only that saved trusted user context on the same organization-locked connection, rechecks the exact active link and verified account, and restores context in `finally`. Import clears that authority. No arbitrary user/address is accepted from staff input.

The OS adds one strict-tenant policy table and one transient preview table. Policy fields are `org_id`, `revision`, disabled-by-default `enabled`, opaque `epoch`, `activated_at`, `template_version`, `last_command` and `updated_at`. Preview fields are organization, random ID, actor, policy revision, review hash, bounded bindings (64 KiB), ten-minute expiry, commit timestamp and creation timestamp. At most four retained previews per organization are allowed; expired transient previews may be removed. They are not import source archives and create no permanent private record of a recipient address. Both tables force tenant RLS and count in normal storage accounting.

`os_participations.delivery_consent` holds a bounded object with copy version `participation-email-v1`, enabled flag, separate consent revision, server timestamp, trusted user/link/version binding and recipient hash. It contains no raw email. Historical base `consent.emailUpdates` remains preference-only and confers no transport authority.

The existing outbox gains separate `participation_revision` and `intent_kind` (`legacy`, `snapshot`, `transition`), delivery-consent revision/copy, trusted user/link binding, recipient hash, policy epoch, template version, opaque relay key, payload hash, attempt/lease/scheduling/status timestamps, safe code and reserved budget bytes. Existing rows are legacy paused/cancelled history. `event_revision` becomes the monotonic per-participation notification sequence; keeping it separate from the booking revision lets activation create a fresh snapshot without reopening an old paused row or changing attendance intent. A live uniqueness constraint also binds participation revision, consent revision, template and intent kind. Payload remains at most 2 KiB and has no address/provider ID. Reserve 8 KiB per live intent, counting the larger of measured row bytes and its reservation toward outbox/logical budgets, so bounded completion metadata can be saved after an external attempt even if ordinary storage later fills. Terminal minimal completion does not depend on creating a new audit event.

The OS helper interfaces are `deliveryRoutes`, `ownDeliveryRoutes`, `queueParticipationTransition`, `suppressParticipationDelivery`, `runParticipationDelivery`, and separate delivery export/validation/restore helpers.
