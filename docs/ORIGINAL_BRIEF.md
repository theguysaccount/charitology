# Original Charitology mega list

Source: supplied by the project owner on October 25, 2026, identifying this as Claude’s original list. This preserves all 66 named items and their descriptions. It is a historical brief, not a claim that every item is operating. Later project rules in STYLE.md govern implementation: charity as a system, no judgment of people, honest evidence, and no ratings of named charities before public method review.

## Programs: Research

- **The Open Problems of Charitology:** a public list of the biggest unanswered questions in doing good, like Hilbert’s famous list of math problems. Fund people to solve them. This is your most memeable flagship.
- **Moral Frameworks Program:** compares how different value systems rank causes (utilitarian, rights-based, justice, religious, community-first) and shows where they agree.
- **Methods Lab:** compares the ways of giving itself, such as direct cash, policy advocacy, research funding, local mutual aid, volunteering, impact investing and prizes.
- **Cause Census:** maps every cause area by scale, neglect and solvability.
- **Evidence Reviews:** systematic reviews of every major intervention.
- **Replication Program:** re-runs famous charity impact studies to see if they hold up.
- **Field Verification:** on-the-ground audits that check charities do what they claim.
- **Failure Lab:** studies charities that failed or did harm, so others don’t repeat them.
- **History of Charity:** what actually worked across centuries, like smallpox eradication, the Green Revolution and Carnegie libraries.
- **Forecasting Tournaments:** people predict which interventions will work, and you score them over time.
- **Red Team:** a standing team whose only job is attacking Charitology’s own recommendations.

## Programs: Funding

- **The Charitology Fund:** one fund that always gives to the current best estimate of what’s best.
- **Charity Incubator:** launches new charities to fill the gaps the research finds.
- **Question Bounties:** cash rewards for answering specific open questions.
- **The Charitology Prize:** an annual award for the best charity, research or idea.

## Programs: People and education

- **Certified Charitologist:** a credential for donors, nonprofit staff and advisors.
- **Charitology Fellowship:** trains the next generation of researchers.
- **University Chapters:** student clubs that spread the idea on campuses.
- **Principles of Charitology course:** a free online course, the “Charity 101” of the field.
- **Donor Advisory:** guidance for major donors and family foundations.
- **Nonprofit Advisory:** helps existing charities measure and improve their impact.

## Programs: Reach

- **Charitology Summit:** an annual conference.
- **Local Charitology:** applies the methods to local giving, like “the best charity in your city.”
- **Time Charitology:** the best use of volunteer hours, not just dollars.
- **Corporate Program:** helps companies redesign workplace giving and matching.
- **Policy Program:** applies the research to government aid budgets.

## Tools: For donors

- **Values Compass:** a quiz on what you care about, which then shows your best-matched charities. This is how you make “all ways” personal.
- **Portfolio Builder:** spreads your giving across causes when you’re unsure which values are right.
- **Charity Lookup:** search any charity and see its Charitology rating.
- **Browser Extension:** on any donation page or GoFundMe, it shows the rating and higher-impact alternatives.
- **Impact Calculator:** shows “what $100 does here vs. there.”
- **Compare Tool:** puts any two charities side by side.
- **Giving Dashboard:** tracks your lifetime giving and its estimated impact.
- **Charitology Wrapped:** a shareable year-end recap of your giving, Spotify Wrapped-style. Very memeable.
- **One-Click Split:** give once and have it automatically divided across your portfolio.
- **Legacy Planner:** builds charitable giving into a will or bequest.
- **Disaster Response Guide:** when a disaster hits, shows where money helps most, fast.
- **Gift Giving:** donate in someone’s name as a present.

## Tools: For nonprofits and funders

- **Impact Measurement Toolkit:** templates that help charities track their results.
- **Cost-Effectiveness Model Kit:** open-source spreadsheets anyone can use and audit.
- **Grant Evaluator:** a scoring framework for foundations reviewing proposals.
- **Due Diligence Checklist:** a step-by-step vetting guide.

## Tools: For everyone

- **AI Charitologist:** a chat assistant that answers giving questions from your knowledge base.
- **Public API:** lets developers build your ratings into their apps and donation platforms.
- **Forecasting Platform:** the home for the forecasting tournaments.

## Info bases: Flagship references

- **The Charitology Encyclopedia:** the wiki of every cause, intervention and concept.
- **Global Charity Index:** a database of charities worldwide with financials and ratings.
- **Intervention Database:** every intervention with its evidence strength and cost-effectiveness.
- **Cause Atlas:** a visual map of the world’s problems and how much funding each gets.
- **The Gap Map:** where the world’s charity money goes compared with where the need is.

## Info bases: Annual publications

- **State of Charity Report:** the yearly “state of the world” for giving.
- **The Charitology 100:** the top charities list.
- **Open Problems Progress Report:** which open problems got solved this year.

## Info bases: Research archives

- **Evidence Library:** every relevant study, tagged and summarized.
- **Failure Archive:** post-mortems of charities and programs that didn’t work.
- **Historical Case Studies:** philanthropy’s greatest wins and losses.
- **Disagreement Register:** where experts disagree and why. Publishing this openly builds trust.
- **Country Profiles:** needs and best options, country by country.
- **Open Datasets:** all cost-effectiveness estimates, uncertainty ranges included.

## Info bases: Thought and teaching

- **Journal of Charitology:** peer-reviewed research, which makes it a real academic field.
- **Giving Traditions Library:** the philosophy of giving across cultures and faiths, including Zakat, Tzedakah, Dāna and tithing.
- **Principles of Charitology:** the field’s founding textbook.
- **The Lexicon:** a glossary of the field’s terms.
- **Myth-Busting Series:** corrects misconceptions such as “low overhead means a good charity.”
- **Methodology Handbook:** exactly how Charitology rates things, fully transparent.
- **Podcast and Newsletter:** the outward-facing voice.

## Later framing from the owner

Build in public on GitHub so contributors can submit pull requests. Study charity as a system, without judgment of the good being done, and use Humanity’s Checklist’s symptoms-and-sources framing.
