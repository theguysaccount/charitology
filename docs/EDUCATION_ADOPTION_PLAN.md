# Education adoption and outreach plan

Status: proposed process, October 28, 2026 UTC. AI-assisted preparation. The teaching kit is an untested editorial draft. No outreach, classroom visit, course proposal, club registration, partnership application or learner study has been carried out by this task.

## What to offer

The public `/learn/` hub separates five functions: choosing a route, a complete 50-minute taster, an eight-meeting university seminar outline, a four-meeting beginner club starter and an educator-led school adaptation. Seven editable Markdown files include a facilitator guide, separate student worksheet, syllabus, club templates, school pack, starting guide and complete pack. The existing ten-unit self-study course and six-session chapter workspace keep their current criteria and storage formats.

The lesson studies charity as a system. Learners map a fictional project, distinguish outputs from outcomes, make counterfactual assumptions explicit, compare moral questions and design feedback. Depth describes the work, never its worth. The six-source framework remains a hypothesis. There is no requirement to donate, select a named charity, adopt a moral lens, reveal personal beliefs, create an account or submit work publicly.

## Routes to test

1. **Instructor-hosted activity.** Ask a willing educator to review the case and its fit within an existing subject. Offer the inspectable lesson before proposing a visit. Ask what local approvals and access arrangements apply. This is a practical editorial proposal, not evidence of the fastest adoption route.
2. **Student-led group.** Ask the campus office for the appropriate public process, or work with eligible students who independently want to organize. Check existing groups first. The kit cannot register a club, recruit from student lists or establish an official chapter.
3. **Faculty-owned course.** Co-design around actual learning needs with a faculty sponsor and department. The sample syllabus supplies material to inspect; the institution determines workload, assessment, credit and approval. Mason CHSS’s [published process](https://chss.gmu.edu/curriculum/submission_process) is one local example, not a universal rule.
4. **School enrichment or an existing club.** Start with an adult educator or the district’s published educational-partnership route. The first artifact can stay on paper. The suggested ages 14–18 range is an adaptation proposal, not a validated suitability or standards-alignment claim.
5. **Learn from teaching networks.** Ask existing philanthropy and community-engagement programs what already works and where a systems-and-evidence activity might complement it. Do not ask funders to steer student grants toward Charitology or use their names as partners.

Philanthropy education already exists. The university guide links The Philanthropy Lab, Learning by Giving Foundation and Campus Compact as different official-source examples. The school guide links the distinct Scottish YPI program. Their funding, resources, membership and institutional relationships are not transferred to Charitology.

## First discovery batch

The private notes prepare **six primary inquiries across six institutions/organizations**: a university community-engagement office, a university philanthropy institute, a school-district educational-partnership team and three teaching/engagement organizations. Two additional drafts are **held alternatives**, one campus-club route and one adult school-club route. Do not send simultaneous duplicate approaches within the same university or district.

The private shortlist records an actual official source and public channel, one question, relevant artifact, route limits and the next check. A role is a routing request, not a claim that a named incumbent has agreed to help. Where an official site supplies a form or a protected contact link, do not guess an email address. Do not use course/club/funding applications, student directories, family support or beneficiary channels for outreach.

## Message structure

Use the chosen sender’s own wording and a project signature, with `hello@charitology.org` as the proposed reply address. Before any send, verify the current official route, recipient/role, fit, source facts and all linked public files. The owner must separately authorize the exact message and destination; this request authorized preparation and website activation, not outbound mail.

A first message should contain:

- The open project and one sentence about the systems-and-evidence teaching question.
- One inspected fact about the recipient’s published work, followed by one bounded question.
- One relevant public lesson or syllabus link, rather than an attachment bundle or software pitch.
- A small optional next step: a resource pointer, educator review or short adult conversation.
- The actual draft limits: no tested learning gain, accreditation, approved course, institutional partnership or required account.

### Student-organizations template

Ask how eligible students can check existing groups and recognition rules. Make the outside project’s role clear. Offer the club guide, not registration on a student’s behalf or a request for student contacts. School activities should be discussed with adult educators.

### Teaching-network template

Ask what public teaching guidance should be consulted before adding another resource. Offer one specific question, not a claim of novelty or a request to promote to members. Learning by Giving’s [contact page](https://learningbygivingfoundation.org/pages/contact-us) expressly refuses personal grant requests and requests to direct student choices toward a nonprofit.

## Review, pilot and learning

Keep the initial review among willing adults. If a host wants to try a session, agree the actual purpose, institution-owned approval route, facilitator, audience, access format, time, costs and stop decision. A resource review is not permission for a school visit, software integration, learner survey, recording or research study. FCPS’s [partner expectations](https://www.fcps.edu/partner-expectations) illustrate why these topics need distinct questions; do not infer an exemption or clearance.

Use fictional cases and learner-owned worksheets for the first proposed activity. An educator can comment on clarity and preparation burden without sharing identifiable student records. Publication of names, quotes, a case study or a recording needs its own agreement. Do not call teacher enthusiasm, attendance or correct arithmetic evidence of lasting learning or better charitable outcomes.

If a later evaluation is warranted, write a separate plan with the host covering questions, measures, comparison, permissions, access, retention and reporting of unfavorable results. This outreach plan creates no learner study or data collection.

## Contact and follow-up notes

Keep private drafts, names, contact history, replies and preferences outside the public repository. Separate draft, authorized, attempted, confirmed delivery, reply, agreed conversation, locally approved session and actually held session. A faculty response does not establish department-wide adoption; a recognized student group does not establish an accredited course.

A proposed workflow is one personalized inquiry through the appropriate official route and, if suitable and separately authorized, at most one follow-up after seven to ten business days. That interval is an editorial suggestion, not tested outreach evidence. Stop on a decline or contact preference. Send no newsletter subscription, automated sequence, calendar invitation or community post as a side effect.

Adult organizers may manually use existing Charity OS Projects/Resources for a fictional planning example. Opening those module links creates no record and supplies no school approval. The lesson and club kit work without software, and this task changes no OS, mail provider, payment, course-progress criterion or learner record.
