# Charitology master plan

All 66 original items are preserved in [the original brief](docs/ORIGINAL_BRIEF.md) and mapped in [the public roadmap](https://charitology.org/roadmap/).

## Shared OS and original-program workspaces

All nine formerly partner-only programs now have distinct local workflows and exports, with institutional-readiness records kept separate from software availability. The [Charity OS research and plan](https://charitology.org/for-charities/os-plan/) compares ten existing systems, records twenty public user discussions and proposes a six-phase shared build. The shared foundation is implemented at [os.charitology.org](https://os.charitology.org), with 26 typed back-office modules, accounts, roles, approvals, files, reports and recovery. Pilot outcomes and the later public-participation/specialist gates remain open. See [the full plan](docs/CHARITY_OS_PLAN.md).

## Available now and institutional work

The 31 previously seeded or designed items now have usable browser implementations or substantial published editions. See [the implementation and validation record](docs/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md).

The original 66 items now have **66 live software or publication functions**; nine institutional programs still need operating partners. Live describes the exact scope below. It does not certify sources, conduct field research, grant accreditation or establish a fund.

A full course, source-linked country data profiles and a nomination lab are available. Independent assessment, locally coauthored narrative profiles and an approved named-charity list remain separate work governed by people. Newsletter signup uses a separate private mail service, explicit email confirmation and unsubscribe controls; every edition also remains freely available online and through RSS. See [the delivery record](docs/EMAIL_DELIVERY.md) for actual acceptance tests and operating limits. A generative AI service is deferred by the owner; source retrieval remains available. See [the current acceptance register](docs/MEGA_LIST_ACCEPTANCE.md) for item-by-item evidence and remaining engineering/operating scope.

## Work that software does not establish

Independent source review; real funds, paid bounties and prizes, field audits, professional credentials, fellowships, chapter recognition, conferences and qualified advisory relationships require the stated people, resources and governance. Public charters preserve the ambition without claiming those institutions exist.
